# Triply Visual Identity v1.0
**Founder Working Document**  
**Date:** 5 October 2026  
**Stage:** Pre-MVP / Brand-system lock  
**Status:** Visual direction approved at strategy level; exact logo geometry should be verified against the original B3 master before production export

## 1. Objective
Triply needs a visual identity that feels like a serious consumer product company, not a dating-app clone, travel blog, or portfolio experiment.

The identity must support the brand strategy already established:
- connection before arrival
- future travel
- anticipation
- Explorer first, Lover second
- accessible-premium
- warm but safe
- modern but not trendy for trend's sake

The existing **B3 logo direction remains the working master direction**. We should refine and systematize it rather than redesign from scratch unless the original source fails one of the core criteria below.

## 2. Visual Positioning
Triply should visually sit between premium travel products, modern social apps, and high-trust dating products.

It should not visually resemble Tinder, Bumble, Hinge, Hostelworld, Booking.com, airline brands, travel agencies, or nightlife apps.

The visual language should suggest:

> **Two journeys moving toward the same place at the same time.**

That is the core visual metaphor.

## 3. Visual Personality
Triply should feel warm, energetic, intentional, contemporary, optimistic, globally relevant, trustworthy, emotionally expressive, and clean enough for product UI.

Avoid loud neon dating aesthetics, heavy luxury cues, childish vacation colors, corporate blue SaaS styling, generic map/airplane iconography, overuse of hearts, and overly gendered styling.

## 4. Core Visual Metaphors
Preferred metaphors:
1. **Convergence** — two paths meeting.
2. **Overlap** — two date windows intersecting.
3. **Movement** — future travel in progress.
4. **Connection** — two people discovering each other.
5. **Destination** — a shared place, not a map-pin cliché.
6. **Anticipation** — the sense that something is about to happen.

## 5. Logo System
### 5.1 Master direction
**B3 remains the primary visual direction.**

The master logo system should contain:
1. Primary logo — symbol + Triply wordmark
2. Horizontal logo
3. Stacked logo
4. Standalone symbol
5. Wordmark-only version
6. App icon
7. Favicon
8. Small-size optimized mark
9. Social avatar
10. Monochrome black
11. Monochrome white
12. Light-background version
13. Dark-background version

### 5.2 Logo criteria
The final B3 master must:
- remain legible at 16–24 px
- work in one color
- remain recognizable without the wordmark
- avoid relying on tiny internal details
- not resemble a heart, map pin, plane, or location marker too literally
- feel active rather than static
- scale cleanly for app icon use
- maintain distinction from major dating/travel competitors

### 5.3 Clear-space rule
Use the height of the core symbol as the minimum clear-space unit.

Recommended minimum:
- full logo: 0.5× symbol height on every side
- app icon/social avatar: internal safe area of at least 12–14%

### 5.4 Minimum size
- horizontal logo: 96 px digital minimum
- standalone symbol: 20 px
- favicon simplified form: 16 px
- print logo: minimum 24 mm width for full mark

### 5.5 Incorrect usage
Do not stretch, compress, rotate, outline, recolor components arbitrarily, add shadows to the logo, place it over noisy imagery without protection, or surround it with heart/plane/map-pin clichés.

## 6. Color System
The palette should balance warmth and energy for connection, sophistication for trust, enough contrast for accessibility, and differentiation from Tinder red/pink, Bumble yellow, and Hinge's muted neutral identity.

### 6.1 Primary palette
**Triply Violet** — `#6C4DF6`  
Use for primary actions, key brand moments, links, selected states, and highlight strokes.

**Triply Coral** — `#FF6B5F`  
Use for emotional accent, match moments, campaign highlights, and subtle warm gradients.

**Deep Ink** — `#15131D`  
Use for primary text, dark backgrounds, and high-trust areas.

**Cloud** — `#F7F6FB`  
Use for app background and soft surfaces.

### 6.2 Secondary palette
- Lavender Mist — `#E9E4FF`
- Warm Sand — `#FFF1E8`
- Soft Rose — `#FFE5E2`
- Slate — `#6E6A78`
- White — `#FFFFFF`

### 6.3 Semantic UI colors
- Success — `#1F9D73`
- Warning — `#D98E1E`
- Danger — `#D9465F`
- Info — `#4E7AD9`

Use semantic colors for function, not decoration.

### 6.4 Gradient direction
Primary brand gradient: **Triply Violet → Triply Coral**.

Use selectively in hero visuals, match states, social assets, and app-icon moments. Do not put gradients on every button or card.

## 7. Typography
Triply needs typography that feels human, modern, legible, warm, slightly distinctive, and production-friendly.

### 7.1 Primary UI/body font
**Inter** or equivalent high-quality neutral grotesk.

### 7.2 Display direction
Recommended free directions:
- Manrope
- Plus Jakarta Sans
- Sora

Prefer a rounded contemporary sans with restrained personality.

### 7.3 Type hierarchy
- Display: 48–64 px desktop / 36–44 px mobile
- H1: 40–48 px desktop / 32–36 px mobile
- H2: 30–36 px
- H3: 22–26 px
- Body: 16–18 px
- Small: 14 px
- Caption: 12 px minimum

Prefer short headlines, sentence case, generous line-height, and minimal all caps.

## 8. Shape Language
Use rounded rectangles, soft corners, flowing path motifs, subtle circular geometry, timeline/overlap modules, and layered cards.

Recommended radius system:
- small: 10 px
- medium: 16 px
- large: 24 px
- pill: 999 px

## 9. Iconography
Use simple rounded line icons with 1.75–2 px stroke and consistent optical weight.

Core icons: destination, calendar, overlap, chat, profile, safety, report, block, notification, trip status.

Avoid over-detailed travel illustrations, emoji-like icons, and a generic plane as brand shorthand.

## 10. Photography
Photography should feel candid, globally relevant, modern, realistic, and social without feeling staged.

Preferred scenarios: solo traveler in a city, café arrival, coastal/urban moments, metro movement, viewpoints, casual conversation, phone-in-hand travel moments, and two people meeting in a public place.

Avoid overly polished influencer imagery, staged sunset couples, hypersexualized people, luxury clichés, generic airplane-wing shots, and stock-photo smiles.

## 11. Illustration
Use illustration sparingly for empty states, onboarding explanations, safety education, and location/date-overlap concepts.

Style: simple vector, soft geometry, brand palette, subtle depth.

Avoid cartoon avatars, childish mascots, and excessive decorative illustration.

## 12. Motion System
Motion is strategically important because the brand is about movement and timing.

Core behaviors:
- **Converge** — two elements move toward a shared point.
- **Overlap** — date blocks slide into overlap.
- **Reveal** — profiles enter after trip selection.
- **Travel path** — subtle line/path movement.
- **Match moment** — soft scale + fade + convergence.

Timing:
- micro-interactions: 120–220 ms
- standard transitions: 220–350 ms
- brand/story moments: 400–700 ms

Avoid bounce-heavy motion, excessive confetti, slot-machine mechanics, and casino-like reward effects.

## 13. Product UI Direction
Triply UI should prioritize:
1. people
2. destination
3. date overlap
4. trust
5. action

### 13.1 Profile card hierarchy
Show photo, first name, age, destination, overlap dates, key interests, short bio, and verification/safety markers where applicable.

**Overlap dates should be visually obvious, not hidden in metadata.**

### 13.2 Trip card hierarchy
Destination first, date range second, status third, then eligible-people/matchability signal and edit/manage action.

### 13.3 Empty state
> **Not enough people here yet.**  
> We’ll let you know when more travelers match your dates.

## 14. Date Overlap Visualization
This should become a distinctive Triply UI element.

Recommended component:

You: `12 ━━━━━━━━━ 19`  
Them: `   14 ━━━━━━━ 21`  
Overlap: **14–19**

This component can later appear in ads, onboarding, social content, match explanations, and profile cards.

## 15. App Icon
The app icon should use the B3 standalone symbol, a simple high-contrast background, no wordmark text, 12–14% internal safe area, and minimal gradient complexity.

Preferred: violet background with white or warm-gradient symbol.

## 16. Favicon
Use a simplified standalone B3 mark, no wordmark, minimal internal detail, optimized for 16×16 and 32×32, and still usable in monochrome.

The favicon must be a dedicated optimized asset, not just a resized full logo.

## 17. Social Avatar
Use the standalone B3 mark on a solid or restrained gradient background with generous inner padding. No wordmark.

## 19. Ad Creative Style
Ads should combine realistic travel image/video, destination/date hook, minimal UI demonstration, one core idea, and a strong CTA.

Avoid fake chat screenshots, generic romantic stock footage, excessive text, and “hot singles” framing.

## 20. Landing Page Visual Direction
### Hero
Human-led destination imagery + visible Triply product card + clear date/destination cue + light motion + one CTA.

### Product sections
Use real UI, date-overlap visualization, restrained gradients, generous whitespace, and real proof once available.

### Trust sections
Use calmer neutrals, stronger contrast, less gradient, and clear safety iconography.

## 21. Accessibility Rules
Minimum requirements:
- WCAG AA contrast
- body text at least 16 px where possible
- visible focus states
- color never used as the only indicator
- reduced-motion preference respected
- tap targets at least 44×44 px
- captions on campaign video
- readable text over photography

## 22. Competitive Differentiation
Avoid Tinder-style red/pink swipe aesthetics, Bumble-style heavy yellow/honeycomb cues, Hinge imitation, and blue corporate travel-booking palettes.

Triply should own:
- violet/coral warmth
- overlap/timing visuals
- convergence motion
- future-trip anticipation

# Founder Conclusion
Triply should look like a product built around the moment when two future journeys line up.

The strongest visual idea is not “travel” and not “dating.” It is:

> **Two people. One destination. Overlapping time.**

That concept should remain visible across the logo, motion, product UI, campaign creative, and brand experience.
