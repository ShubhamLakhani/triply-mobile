## 1. Executive Summary

### Company
**Triply** is a travel dating product designed around future trips rather than current location.

### Core problem
Solo and independent travelers often want meaningful social or romantic connection during a trip, but existing dating apps are usually optimized around where a person is **now**, not where they **will be**. Travelers therefore rely on workarounds such as changing dating-app location, joining groups, using hostel/social communities, or waiting until arrival.

### Core solution
Triply lets a traveler create an upcoming trip using **destination + arrival/departure dates**, then discover compatible people whose travel windows overlap.

The fundamental product loop is:

> **Profile → Future Trip → Relevant Discovery → Like → Mutual Match → Chat**

### Strategic wedge
Triply's core category position is:

> **Future-location matching for travelers.**

The company should avoid becoming a generic travel app, itinerary planner, social network, or local dating clone.

### Current founder decision
**Proceed to a serious, focused MVP.**

The purpose of the MVP is not to maximize feature count. It is to prove that travelers will:
1. create real upcoming trips,
2. find relevant people,
3. express interest,
4. match,
5. start conversations,
6. return.

### Main strategic risk
**Marketplace liquidity.**

Triply requires simultaneous alignment across:
- destination,
- travel dates,
- dating preferences,
- age and other compatibility constraints.

This means a global launch could feel empty even with many registered users.

### Initial operating strategy
**Density before scale.**

Acquire users around specific destination/date cohorts rather than launching as a generic global marketplace.

# 2. Company Thesis

## 2.1 Vision

Build the default way for travelers to discover meaningful romantic and social connections **before they arrive**.

## 2.2 Mission

Help people turn upcoming trips into opportunities for genuine connection by matching them with compatible travelers who will be in the same place at the same time.

## 2.3 Long-term company opportunity

Triply can become the relationship layer attached to future travel intent.

If successful, a user's future trip becomes a social graph:
- who else will be there,
- who is compatible,
- who wants to meet,
- what conversations begin before arrival,
- which relationships continue afterward.

The strongest long-term asset is therefore not a collection of travel content. It is a high-intent network built around **future movement + compatibility + timing**.

# 3. Problem Definition

## 3.1 User problem

A person plans a solo or independent trip.

They may want:
- romance,
- dating,
- companionship,
- someone to explore with,
- a meaningful connection before arriving.

But the available options are fragmented.

### Current alternatives
1. Use a normal dating app after arrival.
2. Pay/use travel-location features to change location before travel.
3. Join WhatsApp/Facebook/Telegram/Reddit travel groups.
4. Meet people in hostels or events.
5. Use travel-social communities.
6. Leave it to chance.

None is purpose-built around:

> **“Who compatible with me is going to the same place during the same dates?”**

## 3.2 Emotional problem

Travel creates a unique emotional window:
- anticipation,
- openness to new experiences,
- temporary freedom from routine,
- fear of loneliness,
- desire for memorable experiences.

That makes the value proposition stronger than a purely functional “find travelers” tool.

# 4. Market Evidence

## 4.1 Online dating

Statista's current worldwide online-dating outlook projects approximately:
- **€2.76B global revenue in 2026**
- **452.1M users by 2031**
- **5.3% user penetration in 2026**

The market is large enough to support niche products, but mature enough that generic dating is not an attractive wedge.

## 4.2 Solo travel

American Express's 2024 Global Travel Trends research found:
- **76% of Millennial and Gen Z respondents** said they planned a solo trip in 2024.
- **60% of solo-travel respondents** planned two or more solo trips.
- **21% of Millennial/Gen Z solo travelers** cited meeting new people as a reason to travel alone.

Important caveat:
The survey population consisted of people who travel at least once per year and met stated income criteria in selected countries. It is directional evidence, not a universal population estimate.

## 4.3 Travel demand

American Express's 2025 research found:
- **77% of respondents** planned the same number or more international trips than the year before.

Booking.com's 2026 research surveyed **29,733 travelers across 33 countries/territories** and emphasized increasing demand for individualized travel experiences.

## 4.4 Interpretation

The market opportunity does not come from combining two giant TAM slides.

The relevant opportunity is narrower:

> digitally comfortable travelers with an upcoming trip who are open to meeting someone romantically/socially before arrival.

That is the market Triply must win first.

# 7. Product Strategy

## 7.1 Core product object

The **Trip** is the central entity.

A trip should contain at minimum:
- destination,
- arrival date,
- departure date,
- status,
- user relationship,
- visibility/safety controls.

## 7.2 Matching eligibility

Initial eligibility should require:
- same destination,
- overlapping travel dates,
- compatible gender/orientation preferences,
- allowed age ranges,
- account not blocked/reported/disqualified,
- basic profile completion.

Advanced compatibility should wait.

## 7.3 MVP feature set

### Must-have
- account creation/login,
- age gate,
- onboarding,
- profile,
- photos,
- interests/bio,
- preferences,
- future-trip creation,
- destination/date overlap logic,
- discovery,
- like/pass,
- mutual matches,
- chat,
- basic notifications,
- block,
- report,
- unmatch,
- moderation/admin tools,
- analytics.

### Deliberately defer
- AI matching,
- video profiles,
- travel bookings,
- hotel/flight integrations,
- maps,
- group travel,
- itinerary builder,
- social feed,
- stories,
- creator economy,
- complex gamification,
- advanced subscriptions,

# 8. Marketplace Strategy

## 8.1 Liquidity equation

Triply's effective supply is constrained by:

> active users × destination overlap × date overlap × preference compatibility × safety/quality eligibility

This makes marketplace density the primary strategic constraint.

## 8.2 Founder rule

**Do not interpret total registrations as marketplace health.**

The meaningful supply metric is:

> **eligible profiles available per active trip**

## 8.3 Launch model

Start with destination/date campaigns.

Example:

> “Going to Bali in November? Meet people going the same week.”

This is superior to generic global acquisition because it:
- concentrates supply,
- clarifies the value proposition,
- makes advertising specific,
- allows cohort-level liquidity measurement.

## 8.4 Destination selection framework

Each candidate destination should be scored on:
- international visitor volume,
- independent/solo travel relevance,
- English usability,
- dating/social openness,
- seasonality,
- traveler concentration,
- safety/regulatory environment,
- acquisition cost,
- existing waitlist demand.

**Bali is a strong hypothesis, not a permanently locked decision.**

Potential long-term moat comes from:

# 11. Trust & Safety Strategy

Safety must be built into product architecture.

## MVP requirements
- 18+ or legally appropriate age restriction,
- reporting,
- blocking,
- unmatching,
- moderation queue,
- spam controls,
- safe trip visibility,
- no exact hotel/accommodation exposure,
- no real-time precise location sharing by default,
- community standards,
- safety guidance,
- account enforcement.

## Future consideration
- identity verification,
- selfie/liveness checks,
- optional verified traveler badge,
- stronger anti-scam systems,
- risk scoring,
- emergency/safety sharing features.

## Founder principle

Do not trade safety for growth.

A dating/travel product with weak trust can lose product-market fit even if acquisition works.

## 13.1 Phase 1 — Founder-led organic acquisition

Channels:
- Instagram Reels,
- TikTok later if/when strategically useful,
- Reddit/community participation,
- travel micro-communities,
- destination pages,
- SEO destination landing pages,
- referral loops.

Current budget principle:

## 13.2 Creative strategy

Campaigns should be destination-specific.

Example:
> “Going to Bali solo this November?”

Then:
> “See who else is going the same week.”

CTA:
> “Meet before you go.”

## 13.3 Acquisition objective

Do not optimize initially for:
- followers,
- likes,
- impressions.

Optimize for:
- real trip created,
- eligible supply created,
- matchable users added.

## 13.4 Paid acquisition

Paid campaigns should begin only after:
- one or more organic creatives show conversion,
- onboarding works,
- destination liquidity is improving,
- event tracking is trustworthy.

Then paid spend can scale validated creative instead of guessing.

# 14. Growth Loops

## Loop A — Destination density
More travelers in a destination → more useful discovery → more matches → better conversion → more referrals.

## Loop B — Match referral
A user receives value → shares Triply with friends/travel groups → adds supply.

## Loop C — Future trip reactivation
User completes one trip → creates another → becomes recurring supply.

## Loop D — Destination content
Destination-specific content → organic search/social traffic → trip creation → more destination data → better content targeting.

## Loop E — Event cohort
Festival/event campaign → concentrated users → stronger liquidity → social proof → future event cohorts.

# 15. Analytics & Metrics

## 15.1 Candidate North Star Metric

**Meaningful trip-based connections**

Suggested definition:
- mutual match,
- overlapping travel dates,
- two-way conversation.

This is more valuable than account count.

## 15.2 Activation funnel

1. landing viewed
2. signup started
3. signup completed
4. profile completed
5. trip created
6. eligible profiles viewed
7. like sent
8. match created
9. conversation started
10. two-way conversation

## 15.3 Marketplace metrics

Critical:
- % of active trips with at least 1 eligible profile
- % with at least 5 eligible profiles
- median eligible profiles per trip
- time to first like
- time to first match
- match rate
- two-way conversation rate
- liquidity by destination/week

## 15.4 Retention

Travel is episodic, so standard daily-retention thinking may mislead.

Track:
- repeat session during active trip planning,
- new trip created after previous trip,
- match/chat return behavior,
- multi-trip recurrence,
- destination cohort retention.

Build for credible production use, but do not prematurely design for millions of users.

# 19. Key Risks

## Risk 1 — Marketplace fragmentation
**Severity:** Critical  
**Mitigation:** destination/date concentration.

## Risk 2 — Trust and safety
**Severity:** Critical  
**Mitigation:** moderation, privacy, verification roadmap.

## Risk 3 — Cold start
**Severity:** Critical  
**Mitigation:** cohort launch and seeded supply.

## Risk 4 — Mainstream dating substitutes
**Severity:** High  
**Mitigation:** own future-trip use case.

## Risk 5 — Weak repeat usage
**Severity:** High  
Travel is episodic.  
**Mitigation:** multi-trip lifecycle and high-value trip experiences.

## Risk 6 — Acquisition cost
**Severity:** High  
Dating can be expensive to market.  
**Mitigation:** organic destination content and concentrated cohorts before paid scale.

## Risk 7 — Gender/supply imbalance
**Severity:** Critical  
Can destroy marketplace quality.  
**Mitigation:** measure and actively manage cohort composition.

## Risk 8 — Scams/catfishing
**Severity:** Critical  
Travelers may be vulnerable.  
**Mitigation:** verification and enforcement systems.

## Risk 9 — Regulatory/privacy exposure
**Severity:** High  
Sensitive profile and location/travel information.  
**Mitigation:** privacy-by-design and legal review before material scale.

## Founder conclusion

Triply's opportunity is credible enough to justify serious execution, but success depends on solving a marketplace problem rather than merely building a good-looking dating app.

The business must be designed around a simple truth:

> **A future trip is only valuable as a matching surface when enough compatible people share the same destination and time window.**

Therefore product, acquisition, analytics, launch geography, and company priorities must all serve liquidity first.

The current strategy is:

> **Narrow the market → concentrate supply → create real connections → measure behavior → repeat → then scale.**
